# Music & Song Framework

**Scope:** A reusable music-composition method for songwriting, instrumental work, hybrid production and experimental music. This is an **audience-neutral and generator-neutral** framework. General protocol lives in [Project Directions](project-directions.md); the repository/campaign distinction lives in [Framework Boundaries](framework-boundaries.md).

## 1. Creative premise

A campaign chooses its own audiences, content boundaries, artistic identity, genres, listening goals, project-specific hard gates and tools. The framework provides options, not fixed answers. **No child age, family-life topic, 50/50 balance, vocalist, lyrics, duration, metaphor, emotional resolution or Suno requirement applies universally.**

Guiding principles:

- **Judge against intention:** observational comedy, documentary remembrance, layered metaphor, associative language, direct expression, abstraction, silence, linguistic play and musical experience can each be sufficient.
- **Form follows what the work is doing:** a chorus, bridge, hook, reversal, narrator, lyric, tonality or semantic meaning is never mandatory.
- **Specificity and abstraction are tools:** prefer memorable detail when detail strengthens the piece, and preserve nonliteral language when it is essential.
- **Musical credibility and appropriate craft:** musical complexity is valuable only when artistically justified. Genres can retain characteristic musical behavior while being deliberately reinterpreted.
- **Emotional honesty without automatic closure:** unresolvedness, tenderness, comedy, strangeness, humor and discomfort may be appropriate. Do not manufacture optimism or difficulty.
- **Purposeful development:** deliberate repetition, contrast, arrangement, timbral change, rhythmic surprise, recurring motifs and silence can carry musical meaning.
- **Campaign-selected constraints govern:** do not transfer hard gates from one creator's project to an unrelated project.

For creative examples without new rules, use [Architecture of Meaning — Idea Bank](meaning-architecture-examples.md). The optional [Campaign Profile Template](../templates/campaign-profile.md) provides a place to record user choices.

### A1. Architecture of Meaning — independent creative dimension

This dimension asks **what a song is doing with its subject and its listener**, independently of topic, audience, narrative form, musical structure, or genre. It is a flexible creative vocabulary, not a compulsory label, ranking, or quota.

Consider three independent axes:
- **Artistic intention:** reveal, observe, celebrate, remember, express, question, amuse, immerse, or play.
- **Relationship to meaning:** direct, symbolic/layered, associative, ambiguous, listener-generated, or intentionally without further subtext.
- **Listener invitation:** understand, notice, feel, imagine, laugh, participate, contemplate, or simply enjoy.

For concrete prompts and contrasting song premises, see the [Architecture of Meaning — Creative Idea Bank](meaning-architecture-examples.md). That reference contains examples, not additional governing rules.

Illustrative approaches (not exclusive categories):
- **Symbolic/layered** — concrete material carries another idea.
- **Pure observation** — particulars are sufficient.
- **Experiential/sensory** — a sensation, setting, or mood is inhabited.
- **Playful/absurdist** — exaggeration, absurd logic, and nonsense are the pleasure.
- **Dramatic/character-driven** — behavior or interaction sustains interest.
- **Direct emotional** — a feeling is expressed openly.
- **Exploratory/questioning** — inquiry need not resolve.
- **Associative/impressionistic** — images or thoughts evoke rather than explain.
- **Linguistic/musical play** — rhythm, words, sound, or repetition can be sufficient.
- **Documentary/remembrance** — a moment is worth preserving without added interpretation.

**Practice:** In Discovery, actively offer a small number of genuinely different approaches when the alternatives could materially open the concept. Selecting an approach upfront is optional; it may emerge, blend with others, or change during writing. Don't demand an underlying theme, metaphor, plot, emotional turn, or interpretation when the song doesn't call for one. Listeners may discover meaning that the author never encoded. An author-directed meaning is equally legitimate when intentional.

**Boundary:** Abstract, experimental, impressionistic, and surreal writing may be nonliteral and nonnarrative, but should offer a perceptible emotional, sensory, comic, or musical foothold for its intended audience. Campaign-chosen audience/access criteria and artistic-quality standards remain in force.

**Evaluation:** Judge success against the song's chosen or emerging artistic intention, not a universal requirement for layered meaning, dramatic progression, subtext, or revelation. Architecture variety is an opportunity and catalog diagnostic, not a song-level constraint.

### T. Audience and Access Fit
- What is the intended audience, and which access or content constraints has **this campaign** explicitly selected?
- Does the work meet its intended degree of intelligibility, accessibility, challenge, ambiguity or sensory intensity?
- If the campaign targets children, apply **its** developmental/content criteria; otherwise do not import them.
- For instrumental or experimental work, lyrical or vocal access may be irrelevant.

### U. Sustained Experience / Replay Value

- Does material reward a return or sustain one encounter, where either is the goal?

- Do not require adult replay value, child singability or any other specific metric for all campaigns.

## 3. Discovery and Materiality Gate

Use the exact behavior and independent-choice presentation required by [Project Directions](project-directions.md). The dimensions above are a **creative palette**, not a mandatory questionnaire. Ask only questions that may change artistic direction, acceptance criteria or implementation. The songwriter makes the choice after seeing neutral alternatives and a reasoned recommendation.

Material starting questions:
1. What experience is this work intended to create, or what question is it meant to inhabit?
2. Which meaning architecture, if any, would help: direct, layered, sensory, documentary, absurd, ambiguous, associative or explicitly without additional subtext?
3. Who is the intended audience, and **which constraints has the campaign actually chosen**?
4. What emotional, melodic, rhythmic, harmonic, timbral or dramatic behavior serves it?
5. What evidence could change the concept, and what needs a real performance or listening test?

Do not demand a conventional storyline, singer, message, subtext, heroic turn or explanation of every image.

## 4. Lite and Full Briefs

Choose **Lite** for straightforward or exploratory projects, **Full** for complex work whose artistic risks and decisions benefit from more detailed specification. Both are source briefs independent of tools.

### Lite Brief template
- Working title and stage;
- Premise, material, topic or sonic idea;
- Artistic intention and architecture of meaning, if chosen (including absence of deeper meaning);
- Intended audience and **campaign-specific** content/access criteria;
- Primary emotional or sensory effect;
- Expressive behavior (narrative, episodic, non-narrative, instrumental, etc.);
- Genre or musical language, rhythmic or harmonic character when relevant;
- Hook, gesture or motif, if any;
- Ensemble, vocal or instrumental design as applicable;
- Key constraints, open questions and evidence needed for review.

### Full Brief extensions (only when material)
- Secondary emotions, perspective and any intentionally unresolved tension;
- Form, transitions, arrangement arc, narrative strategy or non-narrative process;
- Tempo, groove, meter, melodic/harmonic design and specific instrumental roles;
- Timbre, production, texture, density, space, performance behavior;
- Phrase, language, humor, participation, ambiguity and variation when present;
- Target length or duration only where consequential;
- Motif/callback design, stylistic renovation and influences;
- Tool-neutral execution plan, risk, QA evidence and approval record.

The artistic brief owns the **intent**; a generator/DAW/performance **adapter** owns the mechanism used to execute it.

## 5. Evaluation model

Distinguish:
- **Hard gates:** campaign-selected or unavoidable technical/legal constraints, which cannot be overridden merely by a high score. A chosen children's campaign may have developmental and content hard gates; an unrelated instrumental campaign must not inherit them.
- **Soft scores:** audience fit, musical potential, originality, coherence, experiential quality, performer feasibility, replay value, emotional interest, portfolio contribution and other dimensions **only when they matter to this work**.
- **Creative exceptions:** intentional convention-breaking is welcome if it serves the piece and does not violate a genuine hard gate. Novelty is not enough on its own.

Use evaluation to prioritize exploration, **not to automate musical taste**. Do not turn preferences from a sample campaign into universal pass/fail tests.

## 6. Development, pre-execution QA and listening review

1. Capture the initial subject, sound or experience and the source/provenance in the campaign.
2. Explore a few materially distinct architectures of meaning or musical experience without requiring metaphor.
3. Resolve the campaign-selected audience and applicable hard constraints.
4. Prepare the minimum useful Lite or Full Brief.
5. Draft music, lyrics, score, instrumental texture or production plan as relevant.
6. Convert the approved brief to a **tool-specific execution package only if a tool requires it**, using an optional adapter such as [Suno](adapters/suno.md).
7. Run correctness QA and creative QA on the entire applicable package.
8. Perform, generate, record or test and **listen to the actual result** when listening is possible.
9. Revise material defects and check the complete package after material changes.
10. Save provenance, revisions, review findings and learnings with the work in the owning campaign. Promote broadly transferable discoveries to this framework only after generalization.

### Correctness QA — hard checks
- Fidelity to the approved concept/brief;
- **Only** actual campaign-selected audience/content/access hard gates;
- No contradiction across lyrics, score, performance instructions, controls or other applicable channels;
- No required material missing in the translation from artistic brief to execution;
- Tool limits and settings met **only if that tool is used**;
- Avoid duplication: place a fact in the output channel most capable of expressing it (e.g. generator controls versus musical interpretation), while preserving complementary detail.

### Creative QA — pressure test
- Does the piece deliver its intended experience, rather than comply with a universal song formula?
- Does its hook, motif, phrasing, groove, texture or sonic material work in context?
- Does each section or event have a purpose? Is repetition deliberately stable, evolving or disruptive?
- Where appropriate, could a generic line become a vivid image? Where abstraction is the goal, is it retained?
- Is the emotional turn, bridge, resolution or interpretive layer earned **if one exists**?
- Are harmony, meter, lyric phrasing, instrumentation and production credible in the chosen idiom?
- Does the work offer a perceptible listener foothold at the degree the campaign intends?
- Are awkwardness and irregularity deliberate character or fixable problems?
- Has editing stripped away distinctiveness?
- Which questions require listening/performance evidence, not endless paper revision?

**Finding classes:** `Must fix` (constraint violation, contradiction, loss of critical intent); `Should fix` (clear improvement with evidence); `Test in execution/listening` (cannot be settled reliably from a draft).

**Loop:** Draft → correctness QA → creative critique → revise demonstrable issues → full-package QA → test/deliver. Recheck after any material change. Stop when no correctness failure remains and the next suggestions are mostly defensible alternatives, not clear improvements. Never repair intentional irregularity merely for being unusual.

Use the **PASS / CONDITIONAL PASS / FAIL / BLOCKED** status definitions exclusively in [Project Directions](project-directions.md); this section provides music-specific checks, not a second command protocol.

### Post-execution learning
Listen or otherwise inspect the artifact itself, not merely its brief. Note what succeeded, what fell short and what surprised. Maintain the review and listening history in the owning campaign's song dossier. If a pattern is transferable across unrelated projects, propose it as a core improvement; otherwise it remains campaign-specific.

## 7. Optional extensions and ownership

- [Suno Adapter](adapters/suno.md) specifies *optional* generator fields, limits, prompt construction and UI versions; it does not define creative goals.
- [Campaign Profile Template](../templates/campaign-profile.md) defines where independent creators can choose family, instrumental, adult, child, avant-garde or other aims.
- [Architecture of Meaning examples](meaning-architecture-examples.md) are inspiration, not required taxonomy.
- Public [Project Directions](project-directions.md) owns process activation, conflict rules, override, synchronization and final QA status.
- **This document** owns reusable songwriting and music QA standards.
- **Campaign repos** own actual portfolio, concept lists, history, song dossiers, lyrics, settings, audio and personal provenance.
- For history of the source project's original private-campaign decisions, the private campaign maintains a verbatim historical archive; **do not** reconstruct those as compulsory public framework rules.

One canonical owner per rule; run synchronization/de-duplication QA when changing durable behavior.
