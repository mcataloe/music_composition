## Approved Full Song Brief

### Premise, theme, architecture of meaning
The audience experiences an apparently effortless concert; backstage, many people's schedules, names, contracts, labor, and obligations matter. A first-person narrator gradually reveals that he knows this through performance and music personnel management, and built live internal production-use software to support those needs. Later API/integration/software engineering is related by motivation: careful information handling in service of actual people.

- **Artistic intention:** Reveal; supported by observational details and evolving interpretation.
- **Underlying idea:** Technology worth building understands who depends on it; do not preach this as a thesis.
- **Audience:** Adult listeners, musicians, concertgoers, technical/product people, and the Suno hiring team. Outside the normal family-catalog lane.
- **Primary emotion:** Warm, wry recognition with forward momentum.
- **Secondary colors:** Playfulness, modest pride, curiosity, hope, and an open future.
- **Point of view:** Single intimate first-person voice, reveal delayed until verse 2.
- **Resolution:** The musician/engineer relationship resolves; the professional opportunity stays open, not an invented hire or promised office.

### Narrative and lyric design
1. **Verse 1:** Concrete concert arrival and unseen backstage coordination in the same place.
2. **Chorus:** An apparently simple name/date/line holds an actual person; singable image capable of change.
3. **Verse 2:** The narrator played professionally, handled orchestra personnel details, and built an internal operations program; modern software work grows from the same concern for correctness and people.
4. **Chorus:** Same refrain acquires meaning through autobiographical revelation.
5. **Bridge:** One restrained Songkick reference; bridges artist/fan discovery and the real humans behind information. No criticism of Songkick.
6. **Final chorus:** Slightly evolved words: abstract data becomes a person's journey. Avoid triumphalist recruiting pitch.
7. **Outro:** Small open image of another show and another name. Future remains inviting, not resolved.

Language should sound spoken/sung by a person, not an explanatory essay. Use one Chicago image, one brand mention, and selective technology wording. No unsupported anecdotes presented as autobiographical career facts; the concert arrival scene is an imaginative composite, not a documentary claim.

### Musical language, modern treatment, and arrangement
- **Source tradition:** Chicago blues + soul with jazz chords; modern, organic small band, not pop-country mirroring and not generic twelve-bar pastiche.
- **Groove / tempo / meter:** Baseline ~96 BPM, 4/4, human laid-back backbeat, possible lightly swung feel.
- **Harmony:** Blues-influenced 9th/13th/7th colors; occasional borrowed minor-iv, suspended tones, and natural passing movement. Exact chart/key deferred until audio assessment.
- **Melody:** Conversational behind-the-beat verses; refrain widens but stays singable; one brief instrumental motif develops across sections. Complexity felt rather than announced.
- **Vocal:** Mature, warm, slightly textured, natural single adult lead; slightly wry verses and stronger but unforced chorus. First baseline Male; the artistic brief itself does not hinge on vocal gender.
- **Ensemble roles:** Responsive drums, rounded electric bass, Rhodes/Wurlitzer and piano, expressive clean electric guitar, selective horn punctuation and restrained solo violin countervoice. Violin is optional in final mix, not a solo-showcase or cinematic string bed.
- **Texture / arc:** Start lean; conversational instrumental fills, slightly stronger refrain; bridge thins/changes harmonic direction; final refrain fuller with band interplay but not arena-style; quiet outro.
- **Production:** Close human performances, dynamic warmth, present recording clarity. The violin must belong to the blues-soul ensemble if used.
- **Humor:** Small authentic backstage observations, no novelty-tech punch lines.
- **Participation:** Catchy chorus sufficient; no dedicated participation device required.
- **Potential motif:** Name/line as roster entry, musical stave, line of software code, concert listing, and opportunity. Multiple meanings should emerge rather than be explained.
- **Form/length:** Intro → Verse 1 → Chorus → Verse 2 → Chorus → Bridge → evolved final chorus → quiet outro. Target ~2:55–3:20; preserve breathing room if needed in audio.
- **Avoid:** Generic inspirational lyrics, boasting or pleading, gratuitous abbreviations, invented numbers or career achievements, claims of working for Suno/Songkick, invented historical crises, institutional affiliation with CSO/Lyric, or portraying the internal Rockford Access/VBA platform as a cloud SaaS.
- **Success test:** Music holds up without the job context; human stakes first; bridge and closing suggest genuine future collaboration.

### Style / production instructions (849 characters)

```text
Chicago-rooted blues-soul with nuanced jazz harmony, a warm unhurried 4/4 backbeat, light swung phrasing, and a pocket that breathes. Live small-band feel: rounded electric bass, intimate drums, Rhodes/Wurlitzer, expressive clean electric guitar, and sparing horn responses. Start lean, develop a short guitar/keys figure across the song, thin the texture for the bridge, return with richer ensemble interplay, then end quietly. Warm blues 9ths/13ths with selective borrowed minor-iv or suspended colors. Contemporary clarity, natural dynamics, human performance.
```
