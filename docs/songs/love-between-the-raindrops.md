## Origin and premise

A parent/couple-centered love song about affection maintained inside busy ordinary life, especially after work stress, stupid arguments, disappointment, disruptions, and imperfect repair. Focus on concrete glances, shared laughter, quiet attention, and small mutual acts of care rather than grand gestures. Appropriate for young children to hear; adult listeners are the intended audience and children do not require their own story.

**Working title:** Love Between the Raindrops (provisional).

**Originating personal inspiration (shared in discovery):** After performing at many weddings as a violinist, the songwriter remembers an officiant saying that love is not an emotion but a choice. The inscription on his wedding band says **“I choose you for life.”** This is *creative source material*, not yet an approved lyric, hook, title, or requirement to quote the inscription verbatim. The nuanced songwriting interpretation is that feelings exist, but sustaining a relationship involves continuing acts of choice and mutual care.

## Discovery record

### Round 1 — user-approved directions (2026-10-09)

1. **Narrative shape — D:** Anchor the song in one ordinary day, with glimpses of the couple's longer shared history.
2. **Point of view — D:** A single narrator may move naturally among “I,” “you,” and “we,” keeping the relationship rather than a narrator's monologue central.
3. **Visible difficulty — B:** Let genuine irritation, misunderstandings, disappointment, and imperfect reconciliation appear; the relationship should not look frictionless.
4. **Raindrop metaphor — C:** Start from actual rain, then gradually reveal the broader meaning of affection in small intervals amid life's difficulties. This is an intended approach, not permission to force every scene into weather imagery.
5. **Musical world — D:** Keep genre open until the emotional arc is established rather than lock acoustic, soul, soft-rock, or other production choices prematurely.


### Round 2 (2026-10-09)

- Show loving commitment through actions; do not explicitly explain the choice-versus-emotion theme.
- Keep the wedding-band inscription as private creative inspiration rather than a required lyric.
- Include several recognizable disagreements and genuine difficult periods between partners, not merely busy-day annoyances.
- Some conflicts are repaired, some negotiated, and some persist. The partners may develop respectful, workable accommodations without pretending the underlying differences disappeared.
- End on an understated affectionate gesture, possibly a little imperfect or humorous, without insisting on complete reconciliation.
- Preserve the ordinary-day narrative anchor while allowing glimpses of a longer shared history and serious storms.
- Keep mutual respect and boundaries clear; enduring commitment is not a requirement to accept mistreatment.

### Round 3 pending

Select how rain and storms evolve as images, what conflict scenes to foreground, and the song's musical character. Genre remains open by prior decision.

### Established creative boundaries

- Primary emotional lane: warm adult tenderness with observational realism.
- Child-safe listening, without a forced child-access subplot.
- Preserve mutuality and dignity; love-as-choice must not become a demand for one person to tolerate harmful behavior.
- Avoid turning the officiant's observation into a didactic thesis or substituting abstractions for concrete behavior.
- A repair does not have to erase the original disagreement.
- The final hook, the role of the inscription in the lyrics, the intensity of personalization, the ending, and the musical architecture remain open.

### Next material decisions

- How explicitly should the song say “choice” rather than enact it in details?
- Should the personal wedding-band inscription be used verbatim, transformed, or reserved as private inspiration?
- Which scale of real-world pressure and friction should anchor the day's events?
- How resolved should the closing scene feel?
- After these decisions, select musical architecture based on the resulting emotional trajectory.
